## Key Question 1: Long-term price targets from major institutions and analysts (2027 / 2028 / 2030), with assumptions and revision history

### Takeaway
As of September 2026 the credible long-range targets cluster at $300k–$760k for 2030 (ARK bear-to-base, Standard Chartered $500k, Bernstein ~$300k cycle peak in 2029, Bitwise >$1M by 2030 from one exec), with a 2027 range of $150k–$250k (Bernstein, Galaxy, Standard Chartered). Nearly every 2026 near-term target has been cut at least once (Standard Chartered from $300k to $100k, Citi from $143k to $82k, Bernstein YE2026 to $125k), but the 2030+ targets have generally been kept unchanged, with the cuts attributed to slower ETF adoption, the collapse of treasury-company premiums, and stalled US legislation.

### Inferences
- Long-range (2030+) targets have been sticky while near-term targets were slashed, which suggests forecasters treat 2026 weakness as a timing delay rather than a thesis break; the report writer should flag that the 2027–2028 waypoints (Standard Chartered $225k/$300k, Galaxy $250k end-2027) now require a 2.5–3.5x move in 15–27 months from ~$85k.
- The most rigorous frameworks (ARK, Bitwise, JPMorgan) all key off gold/store-of-value share; the dispersion in 2030 targets is driven mainly by assumed institutional allocation (ARK bull) versus pure digital-gold parity (ARK bear, JPMorgan).
- Sell-side track records in this cycle are poor: JPMorgan's ~$170k 2026 implied price, Grayscale's H1 2026 ATH, Bitwise's 2026 ATH, Tom Lee's $200k+ calls, and PlanB's S2F have all missed to date.

### Gaps
- ARK's per-channel penetration percentages (institutional % of global assets, % of gold market cap, etc.) for each 2030 scenario could not be retrieved (ARK model page returned HTTP 403); only the aggregate targets and the two disclosed adjustments (gold TAM $24.4T, EM penetration cut 80%) are sourced.
- No formal 2030 target from VanEck, Fidelity, Galaxy, JPMorgan, or Citi was found; their views are either dateless (JPMorgan) or shorter (Galaxy 2027, Citi 12-month).
- I found no 2026-dated primary statement from PlanB himself; S2F figures come from secondary coverage.

## Key Question 2: Institutional and sovereign adoption (ETFs, corporate treasuries, pensions, Strategic Bitcoin Reserve, states/foreign governments, bank custody)

Institutional channels now hold roughly 12% of supply (ETPs ~1.26–1.3M BTC, public companies ~1.26M BTC) and banks are building in-house custody/trading, but the marginal buyers weakened in 2026: ETF holdings fell ~100k BTC from the Oct 2025 peak and sat below their ~$86k breakeven for 228 sessions, treasury-company buying collapsed to ~5.9k BTC over three months, and no government (US included) has made an open-market purchase.

### Inferences
- The best-supported synthesis: amplitude is dampening (smaller peaks relative to realized cap, ~50% instead of ~80% drawdowns, half the historical volatility) while periodicity persisted through this cycle. For 2027–2030 modeling this implies lower compounding (peak multiples of 2–3x realized cap, not 4–6x) and therefore lower probability of the highest 2030 targets ($1M+) but higher floor confidence (power-law/LTH-cost-basis support ~$58–65k held).
- If the cycle's timing holds, the next halving-driven upswing would run 2028–2029, matching Bernstein's 2029 peak and Standard Chartered's 2028–2030 ramp; forecasters expecting 2027 ATHs (Galaxy $250k end-2027) are implicitly betting the cycle is dead.
