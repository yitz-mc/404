## Key Question 2: Fed funds rate, expected path, market pricing, and effect on BTC

### Takeaway
The Fed under Chair Kevin Warsh hiked 25bp to 3.75-4.00% on September 16, 2026 (12-0), its first hike since July 2023, with the median dot implying one more hike to 4.00-4.25% by end-2026 and no cuts in 2027. The hawkish pivot (telegraphed at Jackson Hole on Aug 28) pushed 10Y yields to ~5%, yet BTC has rallied through it, trading as an inflation hedge rather than a rate-sensitive risk asset.

### Cited Findings
- FOMC, Sept 16, 2026: target range raised 25bp to 3.75-4.00%; vote 12-0. Statement: "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace"; "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has changed little"; "Inflation remains elevated"; the action supports "a timelier return to the Committee's 2 percent goal"; "The Committee will deliver price stability"; Fed continues "maintaining ample reserves in the banking system." No oil/energy reference in the statement. — [Federal Reserve press release, Sept 16, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)

### Inferences
- The pre-hike range was 3.50-3.75% (implied by a 25bp hike to 3.75-4.00%), meaning the Fed had cut ~150bp from the 2023-24 peak before reversing; the 2026 story is a full pivot from expected easing (50bp+ of cuts priced in early 2026) to tightening.
- BTC's ability to rally ~10% in the week after a hawkish hike, alongside gold and with a -0.86 30-day correlation to DXY, suggests the market is currently trading BTC on inflation/fiscal-debasement and yield-curve-control expectations (Treasury buybacks, possible Fed bond purchases) rather than on the front-end policy rate.
- The dot plot's "one more hike then hold through 2027" path removes the 2026-27 rate-cut tailwind that many BTC bull cases assumed a year ago; the bull case now rests on liquidity/curve management and ETF/treasury demand rather than Fed easing.
